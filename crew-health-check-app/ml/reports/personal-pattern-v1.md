## What it does

A second opinion: today's combination of daily readings compared with how this crew member's own readings usually move together.
It looks at all of a crew member's daily readings together and asks whether today's combination is unusual for this person. When it is, the app shows an insight headed "Unusual pattern for you" with the note "More unusual than any day in your recent baseline" and the caveat "Learned from your own readings. Tested only on simulated data. A second opinion only."

An insight is a second opinion. It never sets a region status, never creates or hides an alert, never changes a triage route and never sends a message by itself. The crew may choose to send it to the ground. The crew card shows no number and no percentage; this card explains the score.

## Inputs and settings

Indicators: `systolic_bp`, `diastolic_bp`, `heart_rate`, `body_temp`, `blood_glucose`, `sleep_hours`, `mood`, `reaction_time`. Exercise is left out because today's value is incomplete until the day ends.

- Each reading becomes a z-score against the crew member's own baseline (median and 1.4826 x median absolute deviation, floored, over days today-30 to today-3; section 10). Urgent re-checks, such as a blood sugar re-check that is not fasting, never enter it.
- At least 4 of these readings must be in today. Design value.
- The baseline days are the window days with all of today's readings recorded. With fewer than 14 the model says "Learning your pattern". Design value.

## How it works

1. Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage of the second-moment matrix of the baseline days' z-scores about zero (scikit-learn `LedoitWolf`, `assume_centered=True`). Shrinkage keeps the estimate stable with few days.
2. Today's squared Mahalanobis distance d² = zᵀ S⁻¹ z, solved with a Cholesky factorization.
3. The leave-one-out distance of every baseline day: fit on the other baseline days, then score that day.
4. Notable when today's squared distance is higher than the leave-one-out distance of every baseline day: the score (today's distance divided by the largest leave-one-out distance) is above 1. No fixed cut-off is used; the person's own history is the threshold.
5. Each indicator's contribution z_i × (S⁻¹ z)_i. The contributions add up to d²; one can be negative when readings that usually move together move apart. The app names the three largest positive ones, with the direction of z_i ("higher than your normal" or "lower than your normal").

The app runs the same steps in `src/engine/models/personalPattern.ts`. A test matches it to this script's parity file within 1e-9.

## Simulated evaluation

- 900 fictional histories: 300 for each of the simulator's crew profiles (c1, c2, c3), seed 42.
- Each history is evaluated on one day drawn from mission days 31 to 120, so the 28-day baseline window is full and the histories are independent.
- The clean day gives the false-notice rate. Shifted copies of the same history add a shift to the last 3 days, on top of the same noise, so each shifted day is compared with its own clean twin.
- Sizes: 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, times the simulator's day-to-day noise of each shifted indicator.
- Patterns:
  - `combined`: Sleep and mood lower, resting heart rate and reaction time higher (`sleep_hours` down, `mood` down, `heart_rate` up, `reaction_time` up).
  - `temperature-heart-rate`: Body temperature and resting heart rate higher (`body_temp` up, `heart_rate` up).
  - `sleep-only`: Sleep lower, nothing else (`sleep_hours` down).
- Three ways to notice a day:
  - `model`: This model: notable when today is more unusual than every baseline day.
  - `rulesOnly`: Any of the app's rules on these readings is met today (Python copy of the rules engine).
  - `isolationForest`: scikit-learn IsolationForest fitted on the same baseline days; flags today when its score is lower than every baseline day's.
- Rules the simulation can trigger: R-SLEEP-01, R-TEMP-01, R-TEMP-02, R-GLU-02. Not part of the simulation: R-CO2-01, R-RAD-01, R-RAD-03.
- IsolationForest settings: 100 trees, max_samples auto, contamination auto, random_state 42.

## Limitations

- Evaluated only on simulated data from this project's own simulator. The results show that the method works as designed on that simulator; they do not show that it notices real illness.
- The simulator draws each reading's day-to-day noise independently, so the way the readings move together is simpler than in real physiology.
- A person whose readings are often unusual raises their own threshold, because the threshold is their own most unusual baseline day.
- A slow drift over weeks becomes part of the baseline, by design (the baseline engine, section 10).
- On stable readings a notice still appears by chance now and then. If today were just like the 28 baseline days, it would be the most unusual of 29 days about one time in 29; the false-notice rate measures it on the simulator.
- An insight can repeat what an alert already says.
- minIndicatorsToday (4) and minDays (14) are design values, not fitted.
- The shifts are added for 3 days on top of the simulator's ordinary noise; a real change may build up differently.
- The rules-only comparison runs a Python copy of the app's rules on readings. Rules on habitat feeds, space weather or symptoms are not part of this simulation: R-CO2-01, R-RAD-01, R-RAD-03.
- IsolationForest uses scikit-learn's default settings and is scored on the same baseline days it was fitted on (no leave-one-out, to keep the run short). It is a benchmark, not tuned.
